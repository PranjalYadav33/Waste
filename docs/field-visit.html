CHAPTER 1

Field visit report

1.1 Introduction

Indian cities have moved in recent years from open dumping toward rule-based waste management under the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016. Segregation practice, however, varies substantially between adjacent neighbourhoods. A planned high-rise and an informal settlement may be served by the same municipal vehicle while facing distinct operational constraints. This study therefore follows waste through four stages: generation and segregation within the household; transfer by domestic workers, housekeeping staff or shop assistants to the society gate, street drum or community bin; collection by mini-tipper, compactor or hopper vehicle; and final routing to sorting sheds, compost or biomethanation plants, material recovery facilities, or — where the chain fails — roadside dumps and landfills.

1.2 Objectives of the field visit

The visit was designed to (i) identify the physical points at which segregated waste is remixed; (ii) record bin availability, labelling condition, collection punctuality and handler practice by location type; and (iii) establish the housing stratification used in the subsequent survey.

1.5 Key findings of the field visit

Zone A — delegation failure. Segregation infrastructure exists, but the transfer step (domestic worker to society bag to tipper) reintroduces mixing under time pressure. Zone B — space constraint. Tenements of approximately 100 sq ft cannot accommodate three separate bins, and commercial waste from ground-floor shops shares the same drum as household waste. Zone C — timing and distance failure. A single missed hopper round converts storing households into corner-dumpers, with waste exposed for half a day or longer. These three mechanisms structure the survey questionnaire and the differentiated recommendations in the Solutions chapter.
