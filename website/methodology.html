CHAPTER 2

Project planning and methodology

2.1 Project objective

To determine why household waste segregation fails between the point of generation and the point of municipal collection, by combining official secondary records with a primary survey stratified across three residential typologies.

2.2 Survey design

The study employs a descriptive-cum-analytical design. Primary data were collected through a structured digital questionnaire (Google Form, 10 items); secondary data were drawn from the CPCB Annual Report 2021–22 (Table-1), SBM-Urban MIS and Garbage Free City 2023 dashboards, MPCB quarterly (Q4-2020) and annual (2023) filings, and five published field studies (Delhi ISI, Mulund, IIT-Mandi/WPI, FOSSEE Thiruvananthapuram, Thanisandra).[3][6][4][5]

2.4 Data collection plan and processing

Responses were screened for duplicate timestamps and straight-lining; none were found in this window. Categorical responses were coded numerically (Yes=1/No=0; Always/Sometimes/Never as 2/1/0) in a spreadsheet codebook and analysed in Python for frequency distributions and housing-stratified cross-tabulations. The contamination-rate definition used throughout is CR = (misplaced weight ÷ stream weight) × 100. All primary charts on the Survey page are generated directly from form-1 - Form Responses 1.csv; all secondary charts on the Findings page regenerate from retained CPCB/SBM/MPCB extracts.

2.5 Ethics and limitations

Participation was voluntary and anonymous; no names or addresses were collected. Photographs were taken with consent and faces obscured. Limitations: the sample is a convenience sample and is not representative of the city; self-reported quantities are approximate; observation covers a single season; urban local body portal claims (including the Maharashtra 98.66% segregation figure) are self-certified and are therefore reported alongside independent verification wherever available.
