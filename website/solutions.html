CHAPTER 3 · SECTIONS 3.5–3.6

Conclusions and recommendations

3.5 Conclusions

First, collection coverage is substantially resolved while segregation at the point of handover is not. With 91.8% of waste collected and 53.7% treated, the binding constraint is the quality of segregation where households transfer waste to the collection system, rather than the extent of vehicle coverage.

Second, the mode of failure differs by housing typology, a result consistent across field observation and survey cross-tabulation. Gated societies exhibit delegation failure at the domestic-worker and housekeeping transfer step; dense chawl and market streets exhibit a space constraint, with approximately 100 sq ft tenements unable to accommodate three bins; informal settlements exhibit timing-and-distance failure, in which a single missed hopper round produces corner dumping.

Third, the barriers admit a stable ranking: (i) absence of compartmentalised transport and proximate dual bins; (ii) irregular collection timing with delays of several hours; (iii) illegible or absent signage, excluding handlers; (iv) an unsegregated sanitary and hazardous fraction; and (v) decay of behavioural gains in the absence of scheduled follow-up.

Fourth, household willingness is considerable and currently underutilised. The Mulund survey records 86.36% awareness with 90.91% stated willingness, and the Delhi trial demonstrates a 50.3-point improvement within one week when bins, instructions and a small incentive are provided together — followed by an 11-point decline by week five. The implication is that infrastructure provision should precede further awareness expenditure in high-awareness wards, and that re-audit at approximately five weeks is a necessary component of any intervention.

Recommendations R1–R9

Recommendation R1Report verified segregation alongside portal claims

Require every urban local body plan to present segregation performance in two adjacent columns: self-reported MIS figures and independently verified figures of the Garbage Free City or field-audit type. The 41.3-point discrepancy documented in this study should be treated as a standing measurement item rather than a one-time observation.

Rationale
Self-certified portal data and field verification currently circulate without reconciliation, preventing credible target-setting.
Implementing agency
Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs; urban local bodies.
Monitoring indicator
Revised dashboard template in use within one quarter.
Evidence
Figure F-2.

Recommendation R2Direct processing investment to the six low-processing states

Prioritise material recovery facility and composting capacity in West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh, Rajasthan, Punjab, Jharkhand and Assam, which treat between 22% and 42% of generated waste despite substantial volumes. Generation volume is not predictive of treatment share and should not govern allocation.

Rationale
Management and infrastructure shortfalls, not city size, account for interstate variation.
Implementing agency
State sanitation missions.
Monitoring indicator
Number of commissioned plants by state and year.
Evidence
Figure F-3; Table T-3.1 of the report.

Recommendation R3Introduce four-stream collection with a separate sanitary and hazardous round

Collect wet, dry, sanitary and domestic hazardous waste as distinct streams, with a weekly dedicated round for sanitary and hazardous items (sanitary pads, diapers, glass, medicines and equivalent). Weigh the collected fraction and publish weekly quantities.

Rationale
Sanitary and hazardous waste constitutes approximately 12% of mass and effectively all handling risk; a two-stream design cannot isolate it.
Implementing agency
Urban local bodies with State Pollution Control Boards.
Monitoring indicator
Kilograms per week of separately collected sanitary/hazardous waste.
Evidence
Figure F-5; survey Q7.

Recommendation R4Complete physical infrastructure before further awareness expenditure in high-awareness wards

In wards where awareness already exceeds approximately 80%, allocate funds first to compartmentalised collection vehicles, proximate dual bins, permanent multilingual labels and engineered lids. The IIT-Mandi record of an 11% improvement in correct sorting within three days from labelling and lids alone indicates the expected order of operations.

Rationale
High willingness without corresponding behaviour (Mulund: 86.36% aware, 90.91% willing) demonstrates that information is not the binding input where hardware is absent.
Implementing agency
Urban local body engineering departments.
Monitoring indicator
Change in correct-sorting share within two weeks of installation.
Evidence
Figure F-6; survey Q5–Q6.

Recommendation R5Combine a differentiated user fee with arrival notification and scheduled re-audit

Apply a reduced user fee to segregating households; operate an SMS or WhatsApp notification approximately 15 minutes before the vehicle's arrival (requested by 60% of respondents); and repeat measurement at five weeks to detect the decay documented in the Delhi trial. Where compartmentalised vehicles are unavailable in the short term, schedule wet waste daily in the morning and dry recyclables twice weekly so that a single-chamber vehicle does not combine them.

Rationale
Behavioural gains of approximately 50 points are attainable but decline by approximately 11 points within five weeks without reinforcement.
Implementing agency
Urban local body revenue and information-technology cells.
Monitoring indicator
Segregation rate at week one against week five, by lane.
Evidence
Figure F-6; survey Q9.

Recommendation R6Determine bin numbers and positions analytically

Adopt the Thiruvananthapuram formulation — required bins equal (population × per-capita generation − source-eliminated quantity) ÷ bin capacity — with bin positions determined by least-distance mapping, and publish per-ward fill data. The computation is suitable as a supervised student technical contribution in collaboration with the urban local body.

Rationale
Drum overflow with prolonged exposure in Zones B and C reflects ad hoc siting rather than demand-based provision.
Implementing agency
Urban local body with student technical support.
Monitoring indicator
Overflow-hours per ward per week.
Evidence
FOSSEE Thiruvananthapuram fellowship record; Chapter 1 observation matrix.

Recommendation R7Apply typology-differentiated provisions

Because failure mechanisms differ by dwelling type, a uniform campaign is not indicated. Detailed provisions are tabulated below (Section 3.7).

Rationale
Delegation, space and timing failures require distinct instruments; a single design cannot address all three.
Implementing agency
Corporate social responsibility sponsors; housing-society committees; ward offices.
Monitoring indicator
Change in contamination rate (CR = misplaced weight ÷ stream weight × 100) by lane.
Evidence
Chapter 1 zones; Q1 cross-tabulation.

Recommendation R8Maintain the dataset as a living panel

Re-administer the ten-item questionnaire quarterly, append results to Annexure D, and refresh Figures F-1–F-6 annually from CPCB and SBM releases. Single-round measurement cannot track behavioural decay or verify portal claims over time.

Rationale
Both behaviour and official series change between rounds; continuity is required for inference.
Implementing agency
Student investigator with project guide.
Monitoring indicator
Quarterly appended dataset with version control.
Evidence
Annexure D.

Recommendation R9Conduct a supervised labelling and three-day observation exercise

Affix permanent bilingual (Marathi/Hindi) sorting labels to the residential collection bins of one building and record overflow and contamination over a three-day observation window, using the contamination-rate definition above. This constitutes the student-level field action of the present project.

Rationale
Faded or monolingual labelling was recorded on 2 of 5 bins inspected in Zone A; handler legibility is a necessary condition for correct sorting.
Implementing agency
Student investigator.
Monitoring indicator
Three-day bin contamination log.
Evidence
Chapter 1 photo log.
